Bible reading challenge with friends

Read the Bible daily with your small group or church friends. Agree what to read, everyone checks in when they have read, and the leaderboard shows who kept up.

Your group chooses the reading and uses your own Bible or Bible app. Tadapop only keeps track of who has checked in, and nothing is staked.

01 · SET IT UP

How do you set up a Bible reading challenge?

  1. Agree the reading with your group first: for example, one chapter of Proverbs a day for 31 days.
  2. Open the Check-in Club tab and press Create Challenge. This part of the app needs an account.
  3. Name it and write the reading plan into how to play. People you invite see it before they join.
  4. Choose how long it runs (7 days, 30 days or custom) and how often people check in: every day, or a set number of days a week. Rest days in that plan are not misses.
  5. Turn on proof if you want it: a photo, a video or both, for example of the page you read, seen by all players or only the host.
  6. Share the invite link or code. Once enough people have joined, the host starts the challenge.
  7. Check in each day after reading. Use the group chat to share what stood out and keep each other going.

02 · READING PLANS

Which reading plans can a group start with?

  • Proverbs: 31 chapters, one a day for about a month.
  • The Gospel of Mark: 16 chapters, one a day for 16 days.
  • Psalms: one psalm a day, for as long as you set.
  • The New Testament: 260 chapters, one a day for about nine months.

What do people ask about Bible reading challenges?

Is there an app for a Bible reading challenge with friends?

You can run one in Tadapop’s Check-in Club. A host creates a private challenge, writes the reading plan into how to play, and invites the group. Everyone checks in after reading, and the leaderboard counts how many days each person hit the target.

Does Tadapop tell us what to read?

Your group chooses the passage and reads it in your own Bible or Bible app. Tadapop keeps track of who has checked in.

Can we skip weekends?

Yes. The host can set a number of days a week, for example Monday to Friday, and rest days in that plan are not misses.

How do we know everyone really read?

The host can require a photo or a video with each check-in, for example of the page you read. Beyond that, logging is self-reported and runs on an honor system.

Can strangers join our reading challenge?

No. Only people with your invite link or code can join, and the host can choose to approve each person first. There is no public discovery and no matchmaking.

What does it cost to run one?

Hosting one challenge at a time is free, and joining someone’s challenge is never limited on any plan. Each person needs an account.
